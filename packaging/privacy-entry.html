Peilung (Apple Watch) — Privacy

Peilung is an open-source field navigation app for Apple Watch: a compass that takes and follows bearings, navigation to a point, a distance odometer, your coordinates and saved marks. It runs on the watch alone and makes no network connection of any kind. This section is the canonical statement of what Peilung does and does not do with your data. The app is open source so that every claim here is independently verifiable. The repository is at github.com/excelano/peilung.

The short version

Peilung talks to nothing. There is no server behind it, no account to create, no advertising, no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting and no third-party code. It reads your location and your walking distance on the watch, uses them there, and never sends them anywhere. Its App Store privacy declaration is “Data Not Collected,” because the developer collects nothing and has nowhere to put it.

What it reads

Location, with your permission and only while Peilung is on screen, to show your coordinates and the distance and direction to a target. Compass heading, while the navigation page is showing. Walking distance from the watch's own step count, with your Motion & Fitness permission, while the odometer is running. Peilung does not use HealthKit and does not start a workout.

What stays on your watch

Peilung keeps the odometer reading and where it started, the marks you save with their names and times, the point or bearing you are navigating, your units and north settings, the page you last used, and the last magnetic declination the compass reported. These live in the app's own storage on the watch, are never sent anywhere, and are removed when the app is removed. Deleting a mark, resetting the odometer or stopping navigation removes that item.

What leaves your watch

Nothing. Peilung makes no network request. It has no update check, no analytics service, no crash reporter and no remote logging.

One thing outside the app's control

Apple collects its own figures about installs and, on devices where Share With App Developers is turned on, aggregate anonymous crash logs. Peilung contains no code that touches that data, but Apple may still show the developer aggregate, anonymised numbers. On the paired iPhone the control is at Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements, and it applies to every app, not only this one.

How to verify the claims yourself

The full source is at github.com/excelano/peilung. Search it for anything that opens a network connection, such as URLSession, Network or a URL being fetched: there is none. What it stores is in small files in its Application Support folder, each written through Store.swift, and in its preferences, under the keys declared beside the settings that use them.
