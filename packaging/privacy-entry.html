Peilung (iPhone and Apple Watch) — Privacy

Peilung is a navigation app for iPhone and Apple Watch that works out where you are, which way you face and how far you have gone. Those are personal facts, so this section sets out what Peilung does with each of them. In brief, it uses them on the device in your hand or on your wrist and sends them nowhere: Peilung has no server, no account and no network code at all. Its App Store privacy label reads “Data Not Collected”.

Two apps, kept apart

The iPhone app and the watch app each work on their own and exchange nothing with each other. Everything below applies to each of them separately, on the device it runs on.

Your position

With your permission, Peilung reads your location while one of its pages is on screen, to show your coordinates and the distance and direction to a target. It stops when you leave the app or the screen dims or locks. Location is not used in the background, and the last position the device reported is held in memory only, not written to storage.

Your heading

The compass reading is used while the Navigate page is showing, to turn the dial and to tap the cues that keep you on a bearing. The device's magnetic declination for your area is learned from the first GPS fix and saved, so that true-north readings work later without a fix.

Your steps

With your Motion & Fitness permission, the odometer asks the iPhone or the watch for the distance since you pressed Start. The device counts steps on its own; Peilung only reads the total. It does not use HealthKit, does not start a workout and writes nothing to the Health app.

What Peilung saves

Small files in the app's own storage hold the odometer (its reading and, if GPS was on at Start, where you started), your marks (each a name, a coordinate and the time it was saved) and the point or bearing you are navigating. Its preferences hold your units, north setting, the page you last used and the learned declination. On the watch, one more file, in storage the app shares only with its own widget, holds what the widget shows: the name of the goal and when the odometer started and stopped. Deleting a mark, resetting the odometer or stopping navigation removes that item, and removing the app from a device removes all of it from that device.

Nothing is sent

Peilung contains no code that opens a network connection: no analytics, no crash reporter, no update check and no third-party libraries. Apple, separately, may show developers anonymous install counts and, from people who have turned on Share With App Developers, crash reports. That setting belongs to Apple and covers every app; it is on the iPhone under Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements.

Checking this

Peilung's source is public at github.com/excelano/peilung. The files are written by Store.swift, location and heading come through LocationModel.swift, and steps through Pedometer.swift, all shared by the two apps. This text lives in the same repository, at packaging/privacy-entry.html, and its history there is the record of every change.
